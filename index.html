Evidence

How this model handles claims.

Every relationship drawn in the visualization carries an evidence entry: a short explanation, a strength label, and a reference slot. Where a verified citation has not yet been supplied, the slot is clearly marked as a placeholder rather than invented.

Established association Consistent evidence Emerging evidence

Language throughout uses "associated with," "can contribute to," "is linked to," and "may influence" wherever causation is not firmly established. This model does not treat metabolism as the sole cause of all chronic disease, and it does not rank one mechanism above clinical judgment.

About

A vantage point, not a verdict.

This experience borrows the Copernican shift as a metaphor: when the center of a model moves, the same observations reorganize into a simpler geometry. It is a way of looking at interconnected metabolic risk factors, not an absolute medical claim.

What this model says
  • Common metabolic risk factors are linked and often move together.
  • Underlying processes such as insulin signaling, inflammation, and mitochondrial function may influence many downstream markers.
  • Improving metabolic health is associated with improvement across multiple risk factors.
What this model does not say
  • That metabolic therapy cures every chronic disease.
  • That medications are unnecessary, or that lifestyle change should replace prescribed treatment.
  • That every symptom originates from metabolic dysfunction.
  • That one metabolic intervention fits every patient.
How to use it

Explore the system, follow signals backward, and compare the two vantage points. Then bring better questions to the people who know your history: your own clinicians. Complementary approaches, one patient.
